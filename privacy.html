Privacy policy

For Toddler Launcher, Toddler Parent, Toddler Doodle, Toddler Camera and Caravan Leveller. Last updated 26 November 2026 (evening).

The short version. These apps do not collect, store or share any personal information with the developer or anyone else. There are no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Everything you set up and everything your child creates stays on your device. The only network traffic is explained below: checking GitHub for updates, and the direct, encrypted link between Toddler Launcher and the Toddler Parent app on your own network.

Who we are

The apps are made by an individual developer in England ("we" or "the developer"), publishing as minisonmars. If you have a question about privacy, email minisonmars@proton.me.

What applies to all the apps

  • We do not collect personal information. Nothing you enter or create in the apps is sent to us.
  • No advertising, no analytics or crash-reporting services, and no third-party tracking code.
  • No sign-in or account is needed, so there is no account to delete.
  • Settings and content are stored only in the app's own storage on your device. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android Settings, deletes them.
  • We do not sell or share any data, because we never receive any.

Children

Toddler Launcher, Toddler Doodle and Toddler Camera are designed to be set up by a parent and used by young children. They do not collect personal information from children, have no ads, no in-app purchases, no chat and no links out for the child to follow. Settings, installs and updates are behind a parent PIN. In line with the UK Children's Code (Age Appropriate Design Code), the apps collect no data about children at all.

Toddler Launcher

Stored on the device only: the list of allowed apps and their time limits, screen time and allowed hours, your child's name if you enter it, a wallpaper photo if you choose one, minutes used per app (for the usage report), and the parent PIN or pattern (kept as a scrambled hash, not as the PIN itself). You can save a backup of the settings to a file you choose; that file stays wherever you put it.

Accessibility service (Toddler Guard). Toddler Launcher uses Android's Accessibility Service API so a parent can lock the device to the apps they have chosen. When switched on, it is told only which app has come to the front. If that app is not allowed it returns the child to the toddler screen, and if the notification shade is pulled down it closes it. It does not read the text or content of the screen, does not record keystrokes or anything typed, and does not store or send this information anywhere. A parent must switch it on in Android's accessibility settings, and can switch it off there at any time.

Remote access and the Toddler Parent app. This is off until a parent switches it on. The toddler device and the parent's phone talk directly to each other over your own Wi-Fi or a private hotspot made by the toddler device. Nothing passes through the internet or any server, and the developer never sees it. Pairing needs the parent PIN. After pairing, the two devices share a secret key that is worked out on each device and never sent, and every message is encrypted with it (AES-GCM). The data exchanged is the launcher's settings, limits and usage report, so a parent can view and change them. There is also a simple web page for a browser on the same network, which asks for the parent PIN; it uses ordinary unencrypted HTTP on your local network, so use the Toddler Parent app for an encrypted connection.

Caravan Leveller

Uses the phone's motion sensor to measure tilt, and its compass sensor for the Compass page. Spoken instructions use your phone's own text-to-speech engine, chosen in Android's settings.

What you enter. Vehicle details, sizes, weights, calibration, checklists, packing lists, service records, mileage and reminders are stored on the device only, inside the app. Nothing is sent to us or anyone else. If Android's own backup to your Google account is switched on, Android may include this data in that backup, as it does for most apps.

Photos and files. When you add a vehicle photo or attach a photo or file to a service record, a copy is kept inside the app. Photos are taken with your phone's own camera app, so Caravan Leveller does not ask for camera permission. Files you pick are copied in through Android's file picker, which only gives the app the files you choose. Opening an attachment hands that one file to the viewer app you pick.

Backup files. "Back up to a file" saves all of the above in one file, in the place you choose through Android's file picker (for example Google Drive, OneDrive or the phone itself). The app writes only to that file. Where it goes from there is up to you and the service you chose, under that service's own privacy policy. "Restore from a backup" reads only the file you pick. If you turn on automatic backup, you pick one backup file the same way, and the app updates that one file every day or week when something has changed. It keeps permission to write to that file only, and you can change or stop this in Settings.

Notifications. The app asks to show notifications so it can remind you when something is due. It checks your reminders on the device once a day and after the phone restarts. Nothing leaves the phone.

Calendar (optional). If you turn on "Show reminders in a phone calendar", the app asks for calendar access. It then adds, changes and removes all-day entries for your dated reminders, in the calendar you choose: either a "Caravan Leveller" calendar it makes on the phone, or one you already have. It only reads calendar names (to let you choose) and the entries it made itself. If you choose a calendar that syncs to an online account, such as Google, that account's provider will receive those entries, as with anything else in that calendar. Turning it off removes the app's entries.

Location (Compass page only). If you allow it, the Compass page uses your approximate location (not precise GPS) to work out sunrise and sunset, the direction to TV transmitters and where to point a satellite dish, and to correct the compass to true north. This is worked out on the phone from built-in data. The last location is kept on the phone so the page works offline. Your location is never sent anywhere. The "Check coverage on Ofcom's map" button opens Ofcom's website in your browser, and what you do there is covered by Ofcom's own privacy policy.

Trip diary. Trips you save (dates, site, address, map position, pitch, rating, cost, notes, photos and the site's contact details) are stored on the device only, like the rest of your data. The map's "where am I" button asks for your location, and you can choose precise or approximate. It is only used on the phone, to show you on the map; a site's pin is stored only where you place it. "Find from the address" and "Address from the pin" use your phone's own address lookup (on most phones, Google's), which sends the address or position you're looking up to that service. Call, Email, Website and Directions buttons hand that one detail to the app you use for it.

Sites map. The map of your sites uses map pictures (tiles) from OpenStreetMap, downloaded over the internet and kept on the phone for up to 30 days. To send them, OpenStreetMap's servers receive your device's IP address and which areas of the map are being shown, but not your trips or pins, which are drawn on the phone. See the OpenStreetMap Foundation privacy policy.

Mobile signal. The Compass page shows the network name and signal strength that Android reports. This is not stored or sent anywhere.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law (UK GDPR) you have rights over personal data held about you. Because we do not collect or hold any, there is nothing for us to access, correct or delete. You can delete everything an app has stored by uninstalling it or clearing its storage in Android Settings.
